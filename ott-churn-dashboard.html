01 · Root causes

Why subscribers leave

Two behaviors predict churn far more than anything else. Everything else — age, gender, region, device — is noise.

Watch hours vs. churn rate

r = −0.54

Subscribers watching under 5 hours a week churn at 72.8%. Past 20 hours, that drops to 9%.

Days since login vs. churn rate

r = +0.72

Churn nearly quadruples once a subscriber has been quiet for 30+ days. This is the single strongest predictor in the model.

Churn rate by plan

Basic churns at roughly 1.4× the rate of Standard or Premium — price alone doesn't explain it.

What doesn't move the needle

Age, gender, region and device all sit within a couple of points of a coin flip. None of them meaningfully separate stayers from leavers.

02 · Segmentation

Three ways to lose a subscriber

K-Means grouped the 2,874 at-risk subscribers into three behaviorally distinct segments. Each gets a different save — sized to what they're worth and why they're drifting.

Share of at-risk population

2,874 subscribers flagged medium or high risk, split three ways by root cause.

Assumption set A targeted intervention recovers 25% of subscribers otherwise expected to churn in a segment. Retained value = 12 months of that subscriber's monthly fee. Adjustable against real win-back rates.

04 · How this was built

Methodology

Data

5,000 subscribers cleaned and structured across watch time, login recency, plan tier, monthly fee, and tenure indicators.

Model

Logistic Regression estimates churn probability per subscriber — chosen for coefficient interpretability alongside strong performance (ROC-AUC 0.88, recall 0.84).

Segments

K-Means clusters the 2,874 medium/high-risk subscribers into three behavioral groups by engagement, plan tier, and login recency.

Decision layer

Each segment maps to one retention action, priced with a stated, adjustable assumption set rather than a single blanket offer.

Tools Python (pandas, scikit-learn) for the pipeline · Chart.js for this console · Tableau for the stakeholder dashboard
